# Case Dissection & Strategic Insights: The Dragon Passion Problem at Pulp Riot Juice Co.

## 1. Executive Summary: What Really Happened aligns with the CEO's Verdict

Pulp Riot Juice Co. (**₹1,200 crore** annual revenue, **32%** contribution margin) experienced a classic **synchronized supply chain collapse** during its peak summer season (March–June, which generates **48%** of annual revenue, or **₹576 crore**).

When an influencer reel caused a **4x demand spike** in Quick Commerce for its newly launched **Dragon Passion** blend in mid-March, Pulp Riot’s siloed, monthly spreadsheet-driven planning apparatus failed to detect or respond to the shift in time. Two months later, a predictable **three-week highway closure** cut off the sole passionfruit cooperative, triggering panic air-freighting (**₹3.2 crore**), cannibalization of existing lines, and simultaneous stock-outs of high-demand stock alongside **₹6 crore** in write-offs of overproduced core flavors. Within six weeks, a larger rival stepped into the empty shelf space with a copycat product.

The CEO’s verdict—*"We didn't lose to the competitor; we lost to ourselves"*—captures the core truth of the case: **every single failure was self-inflicted by organizational silos, monthly batch latencies, misaligned functional KPIs, and static allocation rules.**

## 5. Choosing Your Two Deep-Dive Modules: Recommended Pairings & Bidirectional Links

### Option A: Demand ↔ Inventory (or Distribution) — *The "Freshness Cliff & Misallocation" Loop*

* **How Demand Failures Worsened Inventory/Distribution:**

  * Because Demand Planning updated forecasts only monthly (30% error) and missed the 4x QC viral spike for two weeks, production schedules kept running at 95% utilization making core flavors (Mango Peach Basil & Guava Chilli) to match stale forecasts. This pushed millions of unwanted core bottles into depots, eating up the 30-day commercial freshness window (<60 days rejection rule) and causing **₹6 crore in write-offs**, while starving depots of Dragon Passion (**₹46.08 Cr stock-out loss**).

* **How Inventory/Distribution Failures Worsened Demand Planning:**

Unfulfilled orders capped recorded sales in stocked-out depots (censored demand data) while forced discounting of aging stock in overstocked depots artificially distorted sell-through signals, corrupting the month-end consolidated sales data fed back to Demand Planners and worsening their 30% forecast error!

### Option B: Demand ↔ Supply — *The "Bullwhip & Capacity Starvation" Loop*

* **How Demand Failures Worsened Supply:**

  * Marketing gave only 2 weeks' launch notice and hoarded daily QC sell-through reports showing a 4x spike. * **How Supply Failures Worsened Demand:**

### Option C: Supply ↔ Transportation (or Inventory) — *The "Fragile Inbound & Cold-Chain Buffer" Loop*

* **How Supply/Inventory Policies Worsened Transportation:**

  * Procurement’s price-per-kg KPI kept passionfruit single-sourced at a hill cooperative, and Plant Inventory held only 2 weeks of frozen pulp (despite 6-month cold storage life). ## 7. High-Scoring Arbitrage & Decision Logic to Build Into Your Model

To score top marks on **Creativity (20%)** and **Strategic Understanding (30%)**, bake these five concrete, case-grounded mathematical rules into your prototype:

1. **The "Pulp vs. Bottle" Postponement Rule (6 Months vs. 30 Days):**

   * *Case Math:* Frozen pulp lasts **180 days** in plant cold storage; bottled juice has only a **30-day commercial window** before MT/QC buyers reject it (<60 days of 90-day shelf life left).

   * *Model Rule:* Shift safety stock upstream! Before April 15 (ahead of the May landslide window), build passionfruit and dragonfruit **frozen pulp inventory to 5–6 weeks of cover** in plant cold storage (eliminating the ₹3.2 Cr air-freight risk), while tightening depot finished-goods cover from a static 3 weeks (21 days) down to a **dynamic 10–14 days** replenished weekly based on real-time velocity.

2. **The Inter-Depot Transfer vs. Write-Off Arbitrage Rule (₹1.50 vs. ₹60.00):**

   * *Case Math:* Moving a bottle between depots takes **2–4 days** and costs **₹1.50/bottle**. Letting a bottle breach the 60-day freshness threshold in an overstocked depot costs up to **₹60/bottle** in write-offs or heavy discounting (which cost ₹6 crore last summer).

   * *Model Rule:* Whenever any batch at Depot A reaches **Day 18 of age** (only 12 days left before the 30-day MT/QC rejection cliff) AND projected sell-through at Depot A won't clear it in 7 days, the agent automatically recommends an **inter-depot transfer (₹1.50/bottle)** to a high-velocity depot (Depot B) or re-routes it to **General Trade** (which does not enforce the strict 60-day automated rejection rule of MT/QC!).

3. **The Co-Packer Trigger Rule (+25% Production Cost vs. Lost ₹19.20 Contribution Margin):**

   * *Case Math:* In-house variable cost is ₹40.80/bottle (₹60 price − ₹19.20 CM). Even if the co-packer charges 25% more on conversion/production cost, each bottle sold still generates strong positive contribution margin and protects shelf space from competitors.

   * *Model Rule:* As soon as the Daily QC Demand Sensor detects a >25% velocity deviation over a 3-day rolling window, the model projects 14-day plant utilization. If projected utilization crosses 93%, it immediately sends a decision alert to the COO to issue the **2-week notice to the co-packer** (+10% capacity) and reallocates in-house line hours away from high-cover Mango/Guava SKUs.

4. **Dual-Sourcing & Forward Pulp Positioning Math:**

   * *Case Math:* Air freight cost **₹3.2 crore** (₹2.56 Cr excess penalty over normal ₹0.64 Cr road freight).

   * *Model Rule:* Even if qualifying a secondary passionfruit supplier or paying for extra cold-storage holding costs ₹0.40–0.50 crore, it saves ₹2.56 crore in air freight and ₹14.75 crore in lost margin. Replace Procurement’s "lowest price per kg" KPI with **Total Landed & Risk-Adjusted Cost per kg**.

5. **Bridging the Marketing-to-Planner Data Silo (Zero-Cost Quick Win):**

   * *Case Math:* Quick-commerce platforms *already* send daily sell-through reports to Marketing.

   * *Model Rule:* Auto-ingest those daily QC CSV/email reports into the central planning model every morning.
